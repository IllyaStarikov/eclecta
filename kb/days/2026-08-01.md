Caveat researchers concede: negative findings on smaller open LRMs may not generalize to frontier products, and much value comes from surrounding verification software (agentic tooling, DeepMind's AlphaProof Nexus using Lean).

### [Run Kimi K3 using 29 GB of RAM at 0.50 tok/s](https://github.com/sqliteai/waste) — 8/10

Self-reported decode is 0.49-0.54 tok/s on an M5 Pro MacBook Pro, 64 GB, with a 29.05 GB RAM floor at 4K context. I/O runs near the SSD limit (~9.9 of 12.78 GB/s), so it only gets cheaper by cache hits, which means more RAM. · The usable RAM window is narrow and non-monotonic: hit rate climbs with budget, but past ~46 GB on a 64 GB machine the OS pages out the cache, so 58 GB (37% hits) runs 8x slower than 46 GB (13% hits). The default steps down a whole working set at a time and takes the largest fit under 7/8 of RAM. Storage matters: internal NVMe streams; the same token over a 0.94 GB/s USB enclosure takes 13 seconds. · Correctness claims are self-reported against a PyTorch reference: final logits agree to 3.6e-06, the vision tower to 2.3e-06, MLA-absorbed attention to 1.2e-05. MLA caches a 512-wide latent instead of expanded KV, cutting 4K-context cache from 11.25 GB to 0.21 GB. · The engine is not K3-specific: Kimi-Linear-48B runs from a 19 GB container at 10.7 tok/s with a 1.87 GB floor. The authors state they found no other published trillion-scale NVMe-streaming demo but note the repo carries no bibliography and frame it as an invitation for counter-examples; pre-converted containers are promised on Hugging Face.
