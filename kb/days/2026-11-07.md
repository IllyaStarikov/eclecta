### [Dust: Pretraining Transformers Without Backpropagation](https://qlabs.sh/research/dust) — 8/10

If the extrapolation holds, pretraining a transformer no longer strictly requires a backward pass, and the method gets more effective as models grow rather than less, inverting the standard assumption about zeroth-order optimization at scale.

Notes: Mechanism: Dust adds Gaussian noise to each linear layer's output independently per token (node perturbation), so one forward pass evaluates a few thousand 'virtual population' members per sequence; reward-weighted noise averaged over draws estimates the output error, and its outer product with the layer's input gives the weight gradient, the same product backprop forms. Attention internals (Q/K/V, gate, value embedding) are credited via alignment with estimated attention-output gradients instead of token loss directly; the 2L residual mixing scalars still use weight-space ES. · Efficiency claim is an extrapolation, not a measured head-to-head: from 1M tokens up, Dust is stated as 10^3-10^4x more efficient than EGGROLL-Transformer, the authors' own transformer implementation of the EGGROLL weight-space ES baseline. EGGROLL at 16k population (256x more) fails to reach Dust at 64 draws; it stays 0.4-0.6 test-loss above Dust at 1M/10M/20M tokens. · Scaling against belief: at fixed 10M tokens across 2M/7M/38M/243M-parameter models (120x range), loss falls with size from 256 population up; past 1k draws the 38M and 243M models gain ~30% more than the 2M/7M ones. The gap to backprop does widen slightly with model size. Authors reframe overparameterization as a larger, better-conditioned search space. · At 20M tokens the power-law fit puts Dust's limit at 4.431 (95% CI 3.89-4.58) vs backprop's 4.633, but the ladder is still falling at 16k draws, so authors read it as 'gap keeps closing,' not a measured win. Gradient cosine with backprop rises with population at every layer type, tested on backprop-trained checkpoints from 10M to 1B tokens. · Scope and caveats stated by authors: not compute-efficient enough to replace backprop today; main cost at affordable population is interference, mitigated by jittering layer types in separate (cached, partial) forward passes. Setup is GPT-style on FineWeb, 4096-token BPE, 16k-token batch, one epoch, SGD-with-momentum base; an Adam variant helps Dust and backprop but barely helps EGGROLL. · Surfaced on Hacker News at 262 points and 76 comments; numbers are self-reported by the authors (Q Labs) and not independently replicated.

### [Obscure audio codec is a perfect fit for the N64](https://phoboslab.org/log/2026/11/n64-ulc-codec) — 6/10

Libdragon now ships a codec that gives N64 homebrew near-Opus audio quality for ~4% CPU, so recorded music and speech fit alongside gameplay instead of eating most of the cartridge: the Half-Life N64 port used it to pack 2,500 sounds and 27 music tracks into one 64MB image.

Notes: ULC (Ultra Low Complexity Codec) by Aikku93, worked on since 2019, originated as a Game Boy Advance demo playing recorded music at 70kbps on a 16MHz ARM CPU with no vector instructions; permissively licensed. · It is a transform codec like MP3: full-band MDCT (no subband split), but coefficients stored as 4-bit signed nibbles with reserved values for zero runs, long zero runs, quantizer changes, and noise fill, so there is no Huffman/entropy coding or arbitrary bit reads. Coefficient unpacking is ~150 lines of C. · Prior options were poor: Libultra/Libdragon ADPCM gives only 4x compression (a 5-min 44.1kHz stereo track is ~12MB versus 8-64MB cartridges), and Libdragon's RSP Opus decoder is high quality but too CPU-heavy for real-time gameplay. · Porting to the RSP required fitting an IMDCT into 4096 bytes of DMEM; the author moved from float to 16-bit fixed point (Q1.14), choosing a 1024-sample block with 512-sample lap buffer (3072 bytes) over the default 2048, trading a higher noise floor for less DMA and faster compute. · RSP port is ~1400 lines of MIPS assembly handling IMDCT, lapping, stereo interleaving and output scaling, barely fitting the 4096-byte IMEM; written iteratively with Codex translating C to Max Bebök's RSPL language, with the author distrusting GPT-5 for direct RSP assembly. · Self-reported result: 44.1kHz stereo at near-Opus bitrate costs ~4% CPU and ~5% RSP time, complexity ~4x ADPCM; usable down to 16kbps; a sparse seek table (every eighth frame) adds ~20 bytes/sec. Merged into Libdragon's preview branch.
