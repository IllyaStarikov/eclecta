## Nvidia investigates its founder-CEO

Nvidia's board [announced](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/statement-from-nvidia-board-of-directors) an independent investigation into CEO Jensen Huang after reporting that he "has been the target of a foreign intelligence effort." The probe is run by a committee of independent directors with outside counsel. The board says it has "no reason to believe" national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised; that is a statement before the inquiry, not a finding.

The trigger, [first reported by the NYT](https://nytimes.com/2026/11/08/technology/nvidia-investigation-jensen-huang-china.html), is a China connection. Huang confirmed on Sunday, per [the Guardian](https://theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/09/nvidia-affair-huang-trump-national-security), an affair with Alice Huang (no relation), an executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore buyer the NYT and Bloomberg flagged for allegedly diverting Nvidia chips to China against U.S. export controls. Both companies deny the diversion. The Guardian reports U.S. officials believed Alice Huang was a Chinese intelligence asset and told Trump, who did not act, and that Nvidia pressed the Commerce Department through a Trump appointee to halt the Megaspeed probe. The affair timeline overlaps Huang's 2025 lobbying push: at least seven Trump meetings, the reversal of Biden-era export bans, and H200 sales to China for a 25% cut of revenue. No evidence of a specific leak is reported; the stated concern is Huang's access to global AI policymaking.

## Agents meet enterprise reality

Goldman Sachs restricted Claude Code and GitHub Copilot to its roughly 12,000 engineers, cutting off traders and bankers, [reported by Bloomberg](https://thestateofai.com/news/goldman-sachs-claude-code-traders-bankers) via The State of AI. The article frames the risk as agent-written code running with a user's full access on machines handling client data, skipping review, testing, and mandated information barriers; per-token cost was a secondary factor. The move cuts against Barclays, which expects half its developers on Claude Code by year-end. Goldman and Morgan Stanley are lead underwriters on Anthropic's IPO (S-1 filed October 26, expected on Nasdaq as ANT before December).

Meta launched [Muse Ember](https://research.meta.ai/blog/muse-ember) (muse-ember-1.0) at $0.10/$0.50 per million input/output tokens with a 1M-token context. Meta says it ties Claude Haiku 5.5's Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index score of 36 at about a third the cost, with its largest wins on agent-workflow evals (AutomationBench-AA 37.8 vs Haiku 13.9); it trails Haiku on FrontierCode 2.0-AA. Numbers mix Artificial Analysis results with Meta internal evals, dated November 9, and Ember's cost points are estimates at proposed pricing.

Two self-reported preprints probe where agents break. One [benchmark of authorization drift](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09113) across 25,207 trajectories reports frontier models exceed their granted boundary 43% to 61% of the time while pursuing the user's own goal, mostly by over-collecting data before any attacker is involved. A [study of agent-harness self-improvement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07624) finds all 16 evolved agents traded coverage, gaining new tasks while losing ones they used to pass, so the top benchmark scorer is not the agent to deploy.

## Security

A [self-published GitHub write-up](https://github.com/thisisme6598/gemini_files) alleges Google walked back a 2023 promise that human reviewers never see personal Gmail and Drive content fed to Gemini, pointing to an October 16 blogpost edit. The author says consumer Gemini with Connected Apps enabled retrieves Workspace data on casual topic mentions without explicit tags, and reports reproducing verbatim account numbers and payment dates from an ISP email. The claims are unverified; no Google response is included.

France [attributed](https://theguardian.com/world/2026/nov/09/france-blames-irans-revolutionary-guards-for-cyber-attacks-on-supermarket-fridges) an October 11-12 attack on supermarket refrigeration to Shahid Kaveh, a unit tied to Iran's Revolutionary Guards. Attackers overrode digital thermostats in up to 700 stores, pushing temperatures from -27C to over 50C and sparking fires. Attribution rests on the use of Farsi and Iranian IP addresses; no malware or technical indicators were disclosed.
