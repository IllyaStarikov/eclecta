## Verification reaches the compiled code

Google DeepMind [says](https://deepmind.google/blog/introducing-alphaproof-code) it formally verified the implementation of the Signal cryptographic protocol, down to compiled device code, in four weeks. The system, AlphaProof Code, splits the work: humans and models translate Signal's security goals into formal specifications, every spec human-reviewed, and agents built on a model called Gemini Carbon generate the proofs. The run produced over six million lines of Lean, whose kernel auto-checks the bulk; agents flagged about 1% of the codebase for human review. DeepMind frames this as the first time any method has verified a widely deployed protocol's implementation rather than its design. As a discrimination test, researchers reintroduced two previously-fixed Signal bugs into an isolated copy and the system produced machine-checked proofs that both versions were insecure.

The claim is large and entirely self-reported. The post links no proof artifacts, gives no methodology detail, and cites no external audit. If it holds, it moves AI-assisted verification from research demos to the kind of codebase that protects real messages, and sets a four-week baseline against the years such work has taken expert teams.

## SSH under active attack, two ways

A decade-old OpenSSH flaw in its interaction with the musl C library, dubbed *sshole, is being exploited. Bruce Schneier [flagged](https://schneier.com/blog/archives/2026/11/new-openssh-vulnerability.html) the bypass, which lets an oversized password (over 256 bytes) log into musl-based accounts configured to block password auth; Alpine and minimal container images are prime suspects. [Ars Technica reports](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/11/vulnerability-that-lets-hackers-ssh-into-linux-devices-is-under-active-exploitation) Greynoise confirmed exploitation four days after disclosure, by a group it tracks as GT10 deploying a backdoor, TAC Agent, that Greynoise says appears AI-generated and carries Chinese-language comments. The backdoor needs Python3, so GT10 installs it on targets that lack it, a detectable side effect. OpenSSH has shipped a fix.

Separately, a researcher [documented](https://kaba.cx/posts/20261020_Tor) how, for a day in October, roughly 333 of Tor's ~3,000 exit relays, about 10% of capacity, ran the Ebury OpenSSH backdoor and redirected port-22 traffic through 12 interception servers that stole passwords and reinfected targets. Key-only auth and host-key checking were the difference. He detected it by opening SSH between his own servers through each pinned exit; Tor marked the relays BadExit on Oct 4 and all were down by Oct 5, leaving a lasting dip in metrics.

Elsewhere on the attack surface: Notion [disclosed](https://notion.com/help/security-incident-involving-a-third-party-ai-vendor) that inference vendor Fireworks AI was breached, exposing unattributable Notion AI chat transcripts for under 1% of workspaces, and told teams to rotate any credentials pasted into Notion AI. And a freelance engineer [chained](https://dixken.de/blog/never-gonna-print-you-up) 14 bugs into network RCE on Prusa 3D printers, fixed in Buddy firmware above 7.0.0, crediting Claude Opus 4.8 and a fuzzer while noting he had to override the model's repeated claims the exploit was impossible.

## Agents at work, flatly

Two Amazon engineers [report](https://strandsagents.com/blog/amazon-saves-millions-replacing-sonnet-with-strands-decider) swapping Claude Sonnet 4.6 for a 4B Gemma-based "decision model" on a 20-way ticket-classification job, cutting list-price cost from ~$22,200 to ~$500 per million tickets by returning option probabilities instead of generated text. The claims are vendor-reported, with stated preconditions: a closed label set, high volume, and context under a few thousand tokens. Simon Willison [built](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/9/mysterious-beeps) an acoustic triangulation system with Claude Code that located a beeping carbon-monoxide detector to within ~40cm, after the agent surfaced and implemented a 2007 ranging paper.
