## PingCAP breach, as the attackers tell it

An entity publishing as [fulcrumsec.vg](https://fulcrumsec.vg/pingcap) posted a forensic disclosure claiming it exfiltrated 10.6 TB from PingCAP and its TiDB Cloud managed database service, covering the control plane, billing, source code, CRM, and four years of customer connection logs. Every figure is attacker-supplied; the post carries no PingCAP response or confirmation.

The claimed mechanism is the part a practitioner should read. The attackers say GCP service-account keys and an Azure service principal committed in PingCAP's own repositories let them run a control-plane replication feed every six hours for 22 days in September 2026, 88 exports with no detection events. They cite a 2022 internal ticket (SECURITY-22) stating any org member, including a billing-only role, could reset root on every cluster, and allege engineers pasted live production secrets into Jira, including a 314 MB slow-query log holding 8,027 third-party credentials (AWS session tokens, GitHub PATs, OpenAI/Anthropic/xAI keys). A tenant-to-customer table names Booking.com, Shopee, Airbnb, Pinterest, Databricks, and LINE among 42-plus companies, with VPC CIDRs attached. The stage-one release is 128.3 GiB compressed across five archives on torrent and onion links, with more promised and an explicit extortion threat toward two named customers. Treat the whole thing as an unverified attacker communication; the operational lesson (committed cloud keys plus an unfixed privilege-escalation ticket) stands regardless of which figures hold.

## Agents at the gate keep missing

Three papers converge on a point: bolting a review or approval agent onto a pipeline does not reliably secure it. [SecPRBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33509) builds 265 tasks from real bug-introducing commits (Firefox, V8, OpenSSL) rather than fix-derived diffs; across eight frontier models, self-reported recall spans 32.5% to 78.1%, and 68% of misses were the agent flagging the right file but arguing the wrong mechanism. A separate preprint on [agent skills](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19264) profiles 6,309 marketplace listings and reports 70% carry an elevated capability and 50% can run a shell, with the permission model "close to inert": of 6,061 parseable listings only 770 declare anything, and 59% of those understate what the bundle does.

[Caper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40753) proposes a fix: pre-execute each shell command in a snapshotted sandbox, hand an independent approver the observed effects, then commit or roll back. On its own YoloFS-Ext benchmark, where harm hides inside invoked tools rather than command text, the authors report a 7.8% harmful-outcome rate against at least 35.3% for text-only baselines, blocking 1.0% of safe twins. All numbers are self-reported; none address pre-execution latency or irreversible side effects.

## Open weights and efficiency

Baidu released [ERNIE-5.5-Flash-Base](https://ernie.baidu.com/release/ernie-5-5-flash-base), a 280B-parameter sparse MoE (14B active) as a raw end-of-pre-training checkpoint, letting teams skip a 33.5T-token run and spend compute on post-training. The architecture leans on hybrid attention: one layer in seven keeps full-context access, the rest fold every 128 tokens into a single cached entry. Benchmarks are self-reported against Baidu-chosen rivals with no independent check. Separately, Linum trained [P-JiT](https://linum.ai/field-notes/pjit-21k), a 2B pixel-space diffusion model with no VAE, on 64 H100s for about $21K under Apache 2.0; quality is self-assessed with no FID numbers.

## Systems and security notes

A Pivot engine post, [It's the Memory Latency, Stupid](https://pivotlake.io/blog/its-the-memory-latency-stupid), uses perf counters to show a naive GROUP BY pegging a core is 50% stalled on DRAM; radix-partitioning to fit cache cut warm runtime from 3.6s to 2.7s. [IPinfo](https://ipinfo.io/blog/rpki-protects-70-internet-routes) reports RPKI-valid routes rose from 54% to 70% in a year, nearly 60% of the gain from China alone, and argues raw country stats mislead: invalid routes reach a median of 2 collectors versus ~595 for valid ones. And [Matthew Garrett](https://codon.org.uk/~mjg59/blog/p/epics-eac2-v2-seems-fairly-easily-bypassable) argues Epic's EAC2 v2 anti-cheat, built on TPM remote attestation, falls to a ~$20 USB-to-SPI bridge that attaches an external TPM, because PC attestation cannot bind a specific TPM to a specific system.
